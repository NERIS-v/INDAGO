Evidentiary Claim Verification
Claim 1: H. Paul Rico's transition from the FBI to World Jai Alai Security Director
CLAIM: Former FBI Special Agent H. Paul Rico retired from the FBI on May 27, 1975, and immediately became the Director of Security for World Jai Alai (WJA).
SOURCE: House Report 108-414 (Volume 1)
EXACT PAGE/SECTION: Section III.B.3 (Oklahoma), Page 89; and Chronology, Page 622.
DATE: May 27, 1975.
WHAT THE SOURCE ACTUALLY PROVES: Rico retired on May 27, 1975, and shortly thereafter became the Director of Security/Vice President for World Jai Alai.
WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE: It does not prove that Rico's hiring was arranged as a pre-planned conspiracy with the Winter Hill Gang, nor does it establish that parimutuel skimming began on his first day of employment.
WHEN THE INFORMATION WAS KNOWN: May/June 1975 (publicly known and filed in WJA corporate/licensing records).
STATUS: INITIAL CASE DATA.
Claim 2: Connecticut investigators' official information request regarding John Callahan
CLAIM: The Connecticut Statewide Organized Crime Task Force requested background information on World Jai Alai President John Callahan from federal law enforcement in January 1976 but received no information of consequence (silence).
SOURCE: House Report 108-414 (Volume 1)
EXACT PAGE/SECTION: Section III.B.6 (Connecticut), Page 96.
DATE: January 1976.
WHAT THE SOURCE ACTUALLY PROVES: Chief Prosecutor Austin McGuigan and his task force requested information from federal agencies and were met with silence. In reality, the federal government possessed detailed files as of January 1976 alleging Callahan was involved in loan sharking with the Winter Hill Gang, and they shared this information with WJA Security Director Rico while keeping Connecticut task force probers completely in the dark.
WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE: It does not prove a formal, documented conspiracy by the entire FBI to facilitate the skimming, but it does prove active federal protection of their informants (Bulger/Flemmi) by withholding information from state investigators.
WHEN THE INFORMATION WAS KNOWN: Known to Connecticut investigators as a failure of communication in January 1976; the fact that the FBI held and leaked these files was discovered much later (during the 2001–2002 congressional hearings).
STATUS: INITIAL CASE DATA.
Claim 3: Mobile surveillance of John Callahan lying about traveling to Miami
CLAIM: Connecticut task force investigators followed WJA President John Callahan when he left a licensing meeting in Hartford; Callahan claimed he was flying directly to Miami, but surveillance tracked him driving to Boston instead.
SOURCE: House Report 108-414 (Volume 1)
EXACT PAGE/SECTION: Section III.B.6 (Connecticut), Page 96.
DATE: Late 1975 / Early 1976 (prior to May 3, 1976).
WHAT THE SOURCE ACTUALLY PROVES: Callahan lied to Connecticut regulators about his destination and immediately traveled to Boston.
WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE: It does not prove whom he met in Boston on that specific night or that he was delivering skimmed cash on this particular trip.
WHEN THE INFORMATION WAS KNOWN: Known to Connecticut investigators at the time of the surveillance in 1975–1976.
STATUS: INITIAL CASE DATA.
Claim 4: Information from Suffolk County (Dwyer) on Callahan's Winter Hill meetings
CLAIM: Boston Suffolk County Chief Prosecutor Tom Dwyer informed Connecticut Prosecutor Austin McGuigan that John Callahan had deep organized crime connections to the Winter Hill Gang, and that local surveillance had documented him meeting with John Martorano, the Flemmis, and Howie Winter.
SOURCE: House Report 108-414 (Volume 1)
EXACT PAGE/SECTION: Section III.B.6 (Connecticut), Page 96.
DATE: 1976 (prior to May 3, 1976).
WHAT THE SOURCE ACTUALLY PROVES: Suffolk County had active surveillance files proving Callahan met with top Winter Hill mobsters, and they shared this verbally with McGuigan.
WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE: It does not prove that this was an official written intelligence report delivered to Connecticut, but rather a verbal exchange of surveillance findings between local prosecutors.
WHEN THE INFORMATION WAS KNOWN: Known to both Massachusetts and Connecticut local investigators in 1976.
STATUS: INITIAL CASE DATA.
Claim 5: The Scheduled May 3, 1976 Hearing and Callahan's Resignation
CLAIM: The Connecticut task force scheduled a formal hearing for May 3, 1976, to obtain Callahan’s testimony. WJA Security Chief Paul Rico learned of the probe, and Callahan resigned and fled Connecticut’s jurisdiction before testifying.
SOURCE: House Report 108-414 (Volume 1)
EXACT PAGE/SECTION: Section III.B.6 (Connecticut), Page 96.
DATE: May 3, 1976.
WHAT THE SOURCE ACTUALLY PROVES: Callahan resigned from WJA and removed himself from the state's regulatory jurisdiction to avoid testifying. It also proves Rico had found out about the investigation shortly before the scheduled hearing.
WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE: [ADVERSARIAL FLAG] It does not prove that Rico warned Callahan or ordered him to resign and flee. While the existing report asserts Rico "warned" him, the House Report only states that "Rico learned of the investigation shortly before the hearing, and Callahan resigned before the task force could secure his testimony." The warning itself is an extremely likely inference but is not directly proven in the text of the congressional record.
WHEN THE INFORMATION WAS KNOWN: Known to Connecticut in May 1976.
STATUS: INITIAL CASE DATA / COUNTER-EVIDENCE (Callahan's flight successfully halted the Connecticut licensing case against him, showing how the corporate-regulatory thread was broken).
Claim 6: Roger Wheeler purchasing World Jai Alai and suspicion of skimming
CLAIM: Roger Wheeler, Sr. purchased World Jai Alai in 1978. In early 1981, he suspected WJA President John Callahan of skimming parimutuel cash for the Winter Hill Gang, leading him to fire Callahan and launch a company-wide audit.
SOURCE: House Report 108-414 (Volume 1); and Whitey Bulger - Wikipedia.
EXACT PAGE/SECTION: Section III.B.3 (Oklahoma), Page 89; and Wikipedia Section "World Jai Alai".
DATE: March 1981 (Callahan was fired and audit launched; purchase was in 1978).
WHAT THE SOURCE ACTUALLY PROVES: Wheeler purchased the company, grew highly suspicious of mob-directed cash-skimming, fired Callahan, and ordered an independent audit of WJA's operations.
WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE: It does not prove that Wheeler had direct evidence of the hit conspiracy at that time, but rather that he recognized a massive financial drain.
WHEN THE INFORMATION WAS KNOWN: Known to Wheeler and WJA's internal circle in early 1981.
STATUS: INITIAL CASE DATA.
Claim 7: WJA-funded Bahamas entertainment of Boston FBI Agents (May 11, 1981)
CLAIM: WJA Security Director H. Paul Rico utilized World Jai Alai corporate funds to entertain active Boston FBI Special Agents Tom Dowd and Jerry Forrester in the Bahamas.
SOURCE: House Report 108-414 (Volume 1)
EXACT PAGE/SECTION: Section III.B.4 (Florida), Page 92; and Chronology, Page 719.
DATE: May 11, 1981 (recorded date of WJA expense ledger).
WHAT THE SOURCE ACTUALLY PROVES: World Jai Alai paid for the active federal handlers of the Boston office to be entertained in the Bahamas, representing an egregious, documented conflict of interest and financial conduit.
WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE: It does not prove that the agents or Rico discussed Roger Wheeler's impending murder or parimutuel skimming on this trip.
WHEN THE INFORMATION WAS KNOWN: Entered into corporate ledger books on May 11, 1981; discovered by Florida investigators (FDLE Agent Lewis Wilson) decades later during corruption probes.
STATUS: INITIAL CASE DATA / COUNTER-EVIDENCE (undermined the objectivity of the federal agents and explains the extreme reluctance of the Boston FBI to cooperate with local police).
Claim 8: Brian Halloran’s January 1982 attempted cooperation and the Tulsa silo
CLAIM: Winter Hill associate Brian Halloran approached the Boston FBI offering detailed information about a January 1981 meeting at John Callahan's apartment where Bulger, Flemmi, and Callahan offered him the contract to kill Wheeler, and noted that Flemmi told him "Special Agent Rico would set up the murder."
SOURCE: House Report 108-414 (Volume 1)
EXACT PAGE/SECTION: Section III.B.3 (Oklahoma), Pages 89, 92; and U.S. v. Salemme, 91 F. Supp. 2d 141 (D. Mass. 1999).
DATE: January 6, 1982.
WHAT THE SOURCE ACTUALLY PROVES: Halloran provided the FBI with a detailed, eyewitness account of the murder conspiracy, directly naming Bulger, Flemmi, Callahan, and implicating Rico.
WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE: It does not prove that the FBI verified his claims or acted on them; instead, FBI OCG squad supervisor John Morris leaked Halloran's cooperation to Connolly, who tipped off the mob, leading directly to Halloran's execution on May 11, 1982.
WHEN THE INFORMATION WAS KNOWN: [ADVERSARIAL FLAG] This is a major "silo" gap. This information was never known to Tulsa or Florida investigators in 1982. The Boston FBI office departed from standard procedures to ensure the information was virtually inaccessible, preventing local police (including Detective Michael Huff) from ever speaking to Halloran or learning of his claims before he was murdered. It was only known to the public and Tulsa investigators in the late 1990s during the federal hearings.
STATUS: INITIAL CASE DATA (contained in the Boston federal silo but hidden from Tulsa, creating a classic "Graph Hole").
Claim 9: Tulsa Police Department's suspect report (August 1982)
CLAIM: Following his July 1982 trip to Boston to find John Callahan (who was murdered on August 1, 1982, before he could be questioned), Tulsa Detective Michael Huff met with Strike Force Prosecutor Jeremiah O'Sullivan. Huff was told Rico was a "rogue agent," and he subsequently drafted an official report in August 1982 listing FBI Special Agents Paul Rico and John Connolly as associates of the Winter Hill Gang.
SOURCE: House Report 108-414 (Volume 1)
EXACT PAGE/SECTION: Section III.B.3 (Oklahoma), Page 89, 92.
DATE: August 1982.
WHAT THE SOURCE ACTUALLY PROVES: Tulsa police officially documented their suspicions of federal corruption, listing active and former FBI agents as active gang associates due to the systemic blockages they encountered.
WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE: It does not prove that Huff had the direct evidence (like the target sheet) to charge or arrest Rico or Connolly at that time; he only had circumstantial patterns of interference and informal verbal tips.
WHEN THE INFORMATION WAS KNOWN: Known to Tulsa investigators in August 1982.
STATUS: INITIAL CASE DATA (representing the highest point of the local investigation's suspicion before being completely blocked).
Claim 10: John Martorano’s 2002 trial testimony regarding Rico’s target sheet
CLAIM: Winter Hill hitman John Martorano testified under oath that former FBI Agent H. Paul Rico (then WJA Security Director) furnished the hit team with information about Wheeler's habits, specifically "a piece of paper written by Rico with all the information--his phone numbers, addresses" to help plan and execute the murder.
SOURCE: House Report 108-414 (Volume 1); fbistudies.com; and mattofboston.com.
EXACT PAGE/SECTION: Section III.B.3 (Oklahoma), Pages 89, 92.
DATE: May 2002.
WHAT THE SOURCE ACTUALLY PROVES: Martorano confessed to Wheeler's murder and testified that the hit team received a written sheet with Wheeler's addresses and phone numbers.
WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE: [ADVERSARIAL FLAG] Martorano's testimony does not prove he received the paper directly from Rico. Under cross-examination, Martorano admitted that he never met Rico or spoke to him directly. He received the piece of paper from the intermediary, WJA President John Callahan, who told Martorano that the paper had been written and supplied by Rico. Because Martorano subsequently murdered Callahan in August 1982, Callahan was dead and could never corroborate Martorano's claim that Rico was the author. Flemmi’s later plea agreement supported Martorano's claim, but Rico died in custody in January 2004 before the allegation could be tested in his own trial.
WHEN THE INFORMATION WAS KNOWN: Known publicly and to Tulsa investigators in May 2002.
STATUS: BREAKTHROUGH.
Adversarial Summary Findings
VERIFIED FACTS
H. Paul Rico was hired as World Jai Alai Security Director immediately after his retirement from the FBI in May 1975.
Connecticut investigators trailed John Callahan to Boston in early 1976 and discovered he was meeting with the Winter Hill Gang, while the FBI withheld their own loansharking files on Callahan.
Roger Wheeler fired John Callahan in March 1981, launched a parimutuel audit, and was assassinated on May 27, 1981.
Brian Halloran attempted to cooperate in January 1982, naming Rico, Bulger, Flemmi, and Callahan. This statement was actively suppressed by the Boston FBI and never shared with Tulsa.
Callahan was executed in Miami on August 1, 1982, immediately after FBI Agent John Connolly leaked to the mob that Tulsa investigators were searching for him.
John Martorano testified under oath in May 2002 that John Callahan had delivered a target sheet with Wheeler's addresses and phone numbers, stating it originated from Rico.
UNSAFE/UNVERIFIED CLAIMS
Rico Warning Callahan (1976): There is no direct proof Rico told Callahan to flee. The record only states Rico "learned of" the licensing probe and Callahan immediately resigned.
Rico’s Authorship of the Target Sheet (1981): Since John Callahan was murdered by Martorano, Martorano's claim that Rico was the author of the target sheet remains legal hearsay, as Martorano never spoke to or met Rico. Rico died in custody in January 2004 before this could be tested at trial.
The Connolly Trial Jurisdiction: [CRITICAL ERROR IN EXISTING REPORT] The previous report states John Connolly’s May 2002 trial occurred in "Suffolk County Superior Court." This is incorrect. Connolly's 2002 trial and conviction occurred in the U.S. District Court for the District of Massachusetts (Federal Court). His subsequent 2008 trial for the Callahan murder was in Florida State Court.
BEST PRE-EXISTING EVIDENCE FOR THE GRAPH HOLE
WJA Audit Ledger (March 1981): Proves the parimutuel skimming was occurring, establishing the financial motive.
Suffolk County Surveillance Logs (1976): Proves WJA President Callahan was meeting with John Martorano and the Flemmis in Boston.
Rico’s Informant Files (1964–1975): Proves Rico was the personal handler who recruited Stephen Flemmi and protected the Flemmi brothers from murder charges, establishing a pre-existing handler-informant relationship.
BEST HISTORIC BREAKTHROUGH EVIDENCE
The Written Target Sheet (May 2002): Confirmed through John Martorano’s sworn trial testimony as the physical vehicle used to transmit Roger Wheeler's personal addresses and phone numbers to the hit team.
