1. Executive Historical Reconstruction
This case study deconstructs the systemic investigative blind spot—or "Graph Hole"—that existed between Case A (The Connecticut Jai Alai / World Jai Alai Licensing and Regulatory Investigation) and Case B (The Tulsa, Oklahoma Homicide of Roger Wheeler) [203].

Historically, these two investigations proceeded under entirely divergent mandates, jurisdictions, and agencies. Case A was a state-level regulatory and licensing probe conducted by the Connecticut Statewide Organized Crime Task Force (starting in the fall of 1975) focusing on the parimutuel gaming industry, corruption, and organized crime licensing blocks [675, 676]. Case B was a tactical homicide investigation launched by the Tulsa Police Department following the daylight murder of Telex Corporation chairman and World Jai Alai owner Roger Wheeler on May 27, 1981, in Oklahoma [440, 571, 575].

At the heart of both cases sat World Jai Alai (WJA), a highly profitable sports-betting enterprise, and a tightly knit group of individuals: WJA President John "Jack" Callahan, WJA Security Director and former Boston FBI Special Agent H. Paul Rico, Boston FBI Special Agent John Connolly, and Connolly's protected top-echelon informants from the South Boston Winter Hill Gang, James "Whitey" Bulger and Stephen "The Rifleman" Flemmi [396, 444, 571, 573].

During the active lifecycles of both investigations, local state-level probers were completely blinded to the critical "cross-case bridge." Connecticut investigators in 1976 knew Callahan had ties to South Boston mobsters but were met with total silence by the federal government when requesting background information on Callahan's loansharking and skimming activities [444]. Oklahoma homicide detectives in 1981 immediately suspected Winter Hill involvement but were actively stonewalled, misled, and blocked from conducting interviews or polygraphs of Bulger and Flemmi by the Boston FBI office under the pretext of protecting active "top-echelon" sources [439, 440].

The ultimate tragedy of this siloed landscape is that the evidence required to connect the corporate skimming to the physical assassination was already sitting in fragmented, non-communicating databases. The Boston FBI held wiretap logs and informant files detailing Callahan's direct extortion by Bulger and Flemmi, and WJA corporate ledgers logged "consulting" expenses that directly funded federal agents [441, 444, 488].

By reconstructing this historical dataset within the INDAGO master architecture, we demonstrate how an investigator using cross-case entity resolution, relationship matrices, and the "Next Best Evidence" loop would have automatically surfaced a high-relevance structural lead, formalized the hidden bridge, and exposed the conspiracy before the network collapsed into a trail of witness assassinations.
